Selection and attribution

Frequency, duration and intensity are shown side by side because they are not interchangeable. A zone with many short mild events poses a different problem to health services than one with few but prolonged and severe events. Collapsing them into a single threshold count, as most Nigerian heat studies do, loses exactly the information that determines the response.

Are the three dimensions redundant?

Regime classification

Regimes are assigned from the combination of the three dimensions, not from any one of them. This is the classification called for by Objective 1.

Climate zones are the main unit of analysis, because heat is produced by climate. States are still summarised, because health budgets and emergency plans are set at state level, and states that span more than one heat regime are named so that one state-wide advisory does not mislead part of their population.

Which partition explains the pattern?

Variance explained (eta squared) by each zonation, computed on the grid cells. A higher value means that partition separates the variable more cleanly.

States spanning more than one heat regime

For these states a single state-wide advisory would be misleading, because their territory contains more than one heat regime, so their outlooks are issued by zone within the state.

Daily warnings in Nigeria arrive a day or two before the heat, and the seasonal outlook comes once a year. This view fills the gap between them: for each climate zone, the chance of a heatwave in each of the next four weeks, with expected duration and intensity, how far each prediction can be trusted, and what partner agencies could do at each level of warning.

Which model predicts best? (skill vs climatology)

Brier skill score against climatology, tested on past events (higher is better, 0 means no better than climatology). Skill falls with lead time and is lower in the humid south, which is why a map of where predictions can be trusted is published with every outlook.

From prediction to action

Draft trigger levels for illustration. In the study, thresholds are set with NiMet, NCDC, NEMA, the Nigerian Red Cross Society and state ministries of health by weighing missed events against false alarms.
Outlook downscaled from the 0.1° grid (about 11 km) to about 1 km with land cover, vegetation, built-up area and land surface temperature.

This is a demonstrator, not a result. Every number in it is synthetically generated to behave the way the real analysis is expected to behave: hazard rising towards the Sahel, vegetation and health infrastructure falling away from the cities, exposure concentrating where population does. Nothing here has been computed from ERA5-Land, MODIS or WorldPop. It exists to show what the finished system does, and to let an audience interact with it during a presentation.

The boundaries are real. State and national outlines come from the GADM level 1 dataset for Nigeria, 37 first-order units including the Federal Capital Territory, simplified with a Douglas-Peucker pass to about 5,100 vertices so the page stays light in a browser. Every grid cell is assigned to a state by point-in-polygon test against those boundaries, which is how the land mask is produced. For the doctorate the same step would use GRID3 or FAO GAUL polygons; the method is identical and only the source file changes.

What is still approximated. Climate zones are latitude bands here, whereas the study will derive them from the published agro-ecological classification. Coastal detail, inland water bodies and interior holes are dropped by the simplification, so the Niger Delta and Lake Chad margin are coarser than reality. The analytical grid is 0.1 degree, matching the native ERA5-Land resolution in the proposal.

The weekly outlook. The research goes beyond describing past heat. Machine learning models (XGBoost and CatBoost correcting the ECMWF extended-range forecast, TabNet, and a data-driven model such as FuXi-S2S) predict the chance, frequency, duration and intensity of heatwaves one to four weeks ahead, tested on two decades of past events. The best predictions are downscaled to about 1 km over towns and cities and updated every week. In this demonstrator the outlook is a synthetic heatwave building over the Sahel and Sudan savanna, peaking in week two to three, with a weaker humid heat signal in the south-west. The skill values are illustrative, not results.

Who it is for. The outlook is designed with the Nigerian Meteorological Agency, the Nigeria Centre for Disease Control and Prevention, the National Emergency Management Agency, the Nigerian Red Cross Society and state ministries of health, so that staff, supplies, cooling and funds can be in place before the heat arrives, in the way Nigeria already acts ahead of forecast floods.

The Ask HeatSense panel is a deterministic stand-in for the Tier 4 agent. It resolves the place named in your question, queries the in-memory layers, ranks the attribution values for those cells and composes an answer from what it retrieved. It states only values it can retrieve, which is the behaviour the real agent is constrained to. It does not call a language model.
